# Your SOP Is Part of the Product

I used to think of SOPs mostly as documentation.

Now I think of them as part of what we're delivering.

If a technology solution technically works but the people using it can't figure out what they're supposed to do, the job isn't finished. The same is true when the process only works because an SME is standing nearby translating everything.

## Write for the person doing the work

An SOP isn't the place to prove how much the project team knows.

The person opening it usually has a job to do.

They need to know where to start, what they need, what should happen, and what to do when something doesn't go as expected.

The curse of knowing a process well is that obvious things stop looking like information.

## Screenshots are useful until they aren't

I like screenshots. They can make instructions much easier to follow.

They can also age like milk.

Interfaces change. Buttons move. Labels get updated.

That's why I don't want an SOP to depend entirely on pictures.

## Document the decision points

Straight-line instructions are easy.

Real work isn't always straight.

I'm usually more interested in the places where someone has to make a decision. What happens if the expected option isn't available? When should they stop and ask for help? What does success actually look like?

If the SOP only documents the happy path, the first unusual situation sends people looking for an SME.

But routine exceptions should not feel like secret levels in a video game.

## "Just ask Mandy" is not a process

Becoming the SME for a technology is useful. Staying the only person who can answer routine questions is less useful.

That made me realize how easy it is for expertise to become infrastructure.

If I answer the same question repeatedly, eventually I want to know why the answer still depends on me.

An SME should absolutely be involved when expertise is actually required.

"Just ask Mandy" shouldn't be the documented workaround for normal operations.

## An SOP should change when the work changes

Documentation isn't done because the file was approved.

Technology changes. Workflows change. People discover better ways to do things.

If the real process and the written process drift apart, people usually stop trusting the written one.

I want SOPs close enough to the work that they can change when the work does.
