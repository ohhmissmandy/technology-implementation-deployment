# Technology Implementation & Deployment

Practical notes on taking complex technology from planning into real-world use.

This collection started with a question I kept coming back to after becoming an ATM and self-service technology SME:

**How did I get good at implementing technology I knew almost nothing about when I started?**

I came into that role without an ATM background. I had to learn the technology, the operational environment around it, and the people and processes that made it work at scale.

Over time, I realized the lessons I was learning weren't really about ATMs.

They were about implementation.

They were about translating between technical teams and business needs. Building a process when one didn't exist yet. Moving from pilot to scale. Deploying physical technology across distributed locations. Working within regulated environments. Preparing users and support teams. Learning from what happened after go-live.

That's what this collection is about.

## Where this perspective comes from

I developed much of my approach while serving as an ATM and self-service technology SME for a distributed banking network of 740 branches across 17 states.

My work covered roughly 1,000 ATMs along with other physical technology and included requirements, solution planning, hardware and software configuration, firmware and software releases, testing, pilots, logistics, deployment, documentation, training, production change, troubleshooting, support, and continuous improvement.

Because I didn't come into the role already knowing ATMs, a big part of becoming the SME was figuring out **how to learn unfamiliar technology well enough to implement and support it in the real world**.

That meant asking a lot of questions. It meant learning from technical teams, vendors, operations, users, and support. It also meant paying attention to what worked once the technology left the project plan and reached hundreds of actual locations.

That experience changed the way I think about implementation.

Knowing how the technology works matters.

But so does knowing how to get it into the environment, make it usable, support it after go-live, and improve the process as you scale.

My work today extends into broader enterprise technology programs, but those lessons have stayed remarkably consistent.

These articles aren't specifically about ATMs or banking. They're about the implementation and deployment lessons I carried forward from that work.

## A note on the examples

The ideas here come from real implementation experience, but the articles are intentionally written at a broader level.

I don't include confidential company information, internal system details, or customer-specific examples. Some situations are generalized so I can focus on the implementation lesson without turning the article into a case study of a particular organization.

The technology changes.

A surprising amount of the work around making it usable, deployable, supportable, and repeatable does not.
