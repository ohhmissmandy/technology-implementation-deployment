# Training for Tuesday

Training can look great on a project plan.

Materials created. Sessions scheduled. Attendance tracked. Training complete.

Then Tuesday happens.

The person who attended the original session is out. Someone new started last week. A user only performs the task once a month and doesn't remember the part we spent seven minutes explaining during training. Something on the screen changed after an update.

That's why I don't think training for go-live and preparing people to actually use technology are quite the same thing.

## People learn differently when the work is real

Training often happens before someone has much context for what they're learning.

You can show me twelve screens and explain exactly what each one does. If I don't have to use screen seven for another month, there's a good chance some of that information is leaving with the meeting invite.

Once people start doing the work, their questions get better.

I like training that leaves room for that second round of learning.

## Train for the job, not the feature list

Users usually care about what **they** need to do with the technology.

If someone only needs three functions to do their job, I don't necessarily need to take them on a guided tour of every feature available. I'd rather make sure they understand the three things they're actually responsible for and what to do when one of them doesn't behave as expected.

The goal isn't for everyone to become an expert on the technology.

It's for them to be able to do their work.

## "They were trained" doesn't answer every question

I've seen training become a convenient explanation when adoption isn't going well.

But if ten people misunderstand the same instruction, eventually I have to consider the possibility that the problem isn't the ten people.

Maybe the training wasn't clear. Maybe the workflow is confusing. The instructions might not match what users are actually seeing.

Training shouldn't become a shield that prevents us from looking at the experience itself.

## The person who trained everyone will not always be there

New employees arrive. People change roles. Someone who attended the original training is now teaching someone else what they remember from it.

People need somewhere to go later. That might be an SOP, a short reference, a recording, embedded guidance, or something else entirely.

The format matters less than whether it works when someone needs it.

## Adoption happens after the training session

Training is part of adoption, but it isn't the same thing.

You can train someone perfectly and still give them a workflow that makes their job harder.

Sometimes the feedback leads to better training.

Sometimes it leads to a better process.

That's why I keep coming back to Tuesday. Go-live tells you whether people were prepared for launch.

The weeks after it tell you whether they were prepared to live with what you launched.
