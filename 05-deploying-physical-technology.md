# Deploying Physical Technology

Hardware has a way of making deployment problems very literal.

A software problem might be fixable from someone's desk. A hardware problem might be sitting in a box at a location four states away. Maybe it has the wrong configuration. Maybe something it needs wasn't shipped with it. Maybe everything about the device is right and the site isn't ready for it.

Once physical technology is involved, the plan has a new set of questions. Where is it? Is it ready? Is the location ready? Does it have everything it needs? And if the answer to one of those is no, how hard will that be to fix from wherever everyone else is?

## Deployment starts before the hardware ships

A lot needs to be right before equipment ever leaves your control.

Is it the right device? Is the configuration correct? Does it need a software or firmware update? Are the right accessories going with it? Is the asset information accurate?

I've learned that this is a much cheaper place to find a problem than after the box has been shipped.

Staging is also where several parts of an implementation tend to meet. The hardware may be physically ready while the software isn't. A device may be configured correctly but still need something on the back end.

If the pieces don't work together when the equipment reaches the site, the solution isn't ready.

## Try not to create work for yourself later

If something can be configured, validated, documented, or caught before shipment, I'd rather do it there than create another touch later.

Every time equipment has to be unpacked again, reconfigured, reshipped, revisited, or physically handled because something was missed earlier, you've added time and another opportunity for something to go wrong.

That doesn't mean trying to predict every possible problem before anything moves. You can't. It means fixing things at the point where they're easiest to fix when you have the chance.

## The site has to be ready too

Physical technology has to go somewhere. That location may need power, connectivity, space, mounting, access, security, installation work, or another piece of equipment before anything can happen.

The exact requirements change depending on what you're deploying. The principle doesn't.

Find out what the site needs before the equipment gets there.

## Shipping is part of the technical plan

I've never seen much value in treating logistics as administrative work happening beside the implementation.

If the equipment isn't where it needs to be when it needs to be there, you have a technical deployment that can't happen.

Shipping dates affect the schedule. Inventory affects what can roll out and when. Vendor lead times matter. Receiving matters. So do the smaller pieces that have to arrive with the main equipment.

A missing accessory at one location is annoying.

A missing accessory across 100 shipments is a deployment problem.

## Standardize the things worth standardizing

A small hardware deployment can survive a surprising amount of information living in someone's head.

That gets harder to defend as the footprint grows.

I want the repeatable pieces to actually be repeatable. That may mean having a standard configuration, a consistent way to validate equipment, clear asset records, or a checklist that catches things we've already learned can go wrong.

Having a clear standard makes the differences easier to deal with. If I know what a typical rollout requires, I can see much earlier when a site needs something different.

## Physical technology keeps existing after go-live

Hardware gets updated. Something breaks. Equipment moves or gets replaced. Eventually, a newer model comes along.

Accurate asset information matters when someone needs to find a device later. Configuration records matter when Support is trying to understand what they're looking at.

If nobody can quickly answer what device it is, where it is, what setup it should have, or what changed recently, troubleshooting starts with a scavenger hunt.

Hardware gives mistakes geography.

Once you put hardware in a box and send it somewhere, the problems can travel too.
